The Airwaves

Boston is the worked example; the rules of the whole world are in here.

Stand on any downtown corner and you are standing in hundreds of signals at once. A phone is negotiating with a small cell on a light pole. A plane on approach is announcing its position about once a second to anyone with a receiver. A dispatcher is sending a fire engine across town. A bus is reporting where it is, a smart meter is reporting how much power a building used, a key fob is unlocking a car, a stadium full of wireless mics is fighting for the same slice of spectrum as the TV stations, and above all of it, GPS satellites are broadcasting the time.

None of it is visible, and almost none of it is accidental. The radio spectrum is a public resource carved up by law: national regulators decide who may transmit where and at what power, countries coordinate through the International Telecommunication Union so they do not drown each other out,1 and the most valuable slices are auctioned - the US C-band auction that closed in January 2021 drew over $80.9 billion in gross bids.2 This guide explains what each signal is, who put it there, how it works, and what the law says about listening - with US rules first and the UK, EU, Canada, Australia and Japan alongside where they differ. To identify something you can see, start at the lookup.

A few ground rules. This guide explains how things work and what the law allows; it never explains how to jam, spoof, decrypt or transmit without a license, all of which are illegal in the US and most of the world. Where a topic is argued over - police radio encryption, scanner laws, health worries about RF - we lay out who says what and attribute it. Sentences without a footnote that explain how something works are our own explanation; everything factual is footnoted. Rules and numbers are given "as of October 2026". If you work in RF and something here is wrong, the corrections desk is at the bottom of every page.

    FAQQuick answers

    How does Flightradar24 know where planes are?

    Flightradar24 says its main source is ADS-B, the position broadcast most aircraft send about once a second, picked up by more than 50,000 ground receivers, most of them user-built. For aircraft with only Mode S transponders it uses multilateration, and it adds satellite ADS-B over oceans and some radar data.

    Is it legal to listen to air traffic control?

    In the US, yes: 18 USC 2511 says it is not unlawful to intercept communications transmitted by any marine or aeronautical communications system. In the UK, a 2012 petition to Parliament stated that listening to services you are not authorized to receive is an offence, though that is a petitioner's statement, not an official ruling.

    Is it legal to own a police scanner?

    In the US, generally yes: the FCC says Section 705 of the Communications Act does not prohibit the mere interception of radio communications. Divulging or profiting from what you hear can violate it, scanners able to receive cellular frequencies are banned, and some states restrict scanners in vehicles.

    Why can't I hear the police on my scanner anymore?

    The agency may have moved to digital P25, moved to P25 Phase 2, which many older scanners cannot decode, or encrypted its traffic. A newer scanner helps with the first two; encrypted traffic is not readily accessible to the public under the Wiretap Act.

    Is Boston police radio encrypted?

    Yes. Boston Police switched to a digital encrypted radio system on August 9, 2025. The department offers a free public feed at radio.rapidsos.com/boston, delayed about 5 minutes in most cases.

    Can scanners pick up cell phone calls?

    US law bars the FCC from authorizing scanners that can receive cellular frequencies, and such scanners could not be made or imported for US use after April 26, 1994. Intercepting phone calls can also bring severe criminal penalties under 18 U.S.C. 2510-2520.

    Why does my phone lose signal inside buildings?

    Walls, floors and windows absorb or reflect radio waves, and the loss generally rises with frequency. Energy-saving low-E glass is especially hard on signals: one industry summary puts it at about 25-35 dB of loss at 3.5 GHz, against 5-8 dB for ordinary glass.

    What does FCC Part 15 mean on my device label?

    It means the device may operate without a license under the FCC's Part 15 rules. In exchange, it has no right to any frequency, must not cause harmful interference, and must accept interference from other users.

    What does "must accept any interference received" mean on an FCC label?

    Under FCC Part 15 (47 CFR 15.5), unlicensed devices have no right to any frequency and must accept interference from licensed stations, other devices and ISM equipment. They must also not cause harmful interference, and must stop operating if the FCC says they are.

    What is the frequency range of AM and FM radio?

    In the US, AM stations are assigned 540-1600 kHz in 10 kHz steps, and FM runs from 88 to 108 MHz with channels 200 kHz apart starting at 88.1 MHz. That spacing is why US FM frequencies end in an odd decimal.

    Does the MBTA subway have cell service?

    Yes. The MBTA has had a distributed antenna system in its tunnels since a 2005 contract, built out line by line around 2010-2012, and American Tower later modernized it for 5G.

    Do I need a license for a wireless microphone?

    In the US, not necessarily. Unlicensed mics may operate in the TV bands at up to 50 mW EIRP under Part 15, must not cause harmful interference and must accept interference. Part 74 licenses, with more power, are open to broadcasters, film and TV producers, and large venues and sound companies using 50 or more devices.

    What is the SAR limit for cell phones?

    In the US, the FCC limit is 1.6 W/kg averaged over any 1 gram of tissue. ICNIRP's 2020 guidelines set 2 W/kg over 10 grams for head and torso. The two use different averaging masses, so they are not directly comparable.

    What did the 2024 WHO review find about phones and brain cancer?

    The WHO-commissioned review led by Ken Karipidis, published in Environment International in 2024, found moderate-certainty evidence that mobile phone use likely does not increase the risk of glioma, meningioma, acoustic neuroma and other head tumors. It screened over 5,000 studies and included 63, according to ARPANSA.

    Why does my Wi-Fi drop when the microwave is on?

    A microwave oven runs at about 1,000 W at 2.45 GHz, inside the same band as 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi, and some energy leaks around the door. A 2009 Design News test found an oven cut throughput by 21% on Wi-Fi channel 1 and 51% on channel 9. Using the 5 or 6 GHz band avoids the problem.

    How accurate is GPS on my phone?

    GPS.gov says GPS-enabled smartphones are typically accurate to within 4.9 m (16 ft) under open sky. Accuracy worsens near buildings, bridges and trees.

    Why is my GPS wrong downtown?

    Tall buildings block satellites and reflect their signals (multipath), both listed by GPS.gov as causes of reduced accuracy. Fewer clear signals and bounced ones shift your computed position.

    Are GPS jammers illegal?

    In the US, yes. The FCC says jammers have no lawful use in the United States and are per se illegal, citing federal law against willful interference and rules barring the sale or use of noncompliant devices.

    What is WWVB?

    WWVB is a NIST radio station near Fort Collins, Colorado that broadcasts a time code on 60 kHz. Radio-controlled ('atomic') clocks typically sync to it overnight.

    Who owns the radio spectrum?

    Governments manage it rather than own it outright. In the US, the FCC administers spectrum for non-federal users and NTIA for federal government users, and many bands are shared between the two.

    How much did the C-band auction raise?

    The FCC's C-band auction (Auction 107) closed its clock phase on 15 January 2021 at over $80.9 billion in gross bids, for 280 MHz at 3.7-3.98 GHz. The previous record was the 2015 AWS-3 auction at $44.9 billion.

    Why can I hear WBZ 1030 at night in other states?

    WBZ is a Class A clear-channel AM station running 50,000 watts day and night from Hull. At night AM signals bounce off the upper atmosphere, and Fybush reported in 2005 that WBZ reached New York City strongly after dark.

    How do I find out who owns a tower near me?

    Search the FCC's free Antenna Structure Registration database by coordinates and radius; it covers structures over 200 ft or near airports. Shorter antennas are better found through license records in the FCC's Universal Licensing System.

    What frequency is NOAA weather radio in Boston?

    The Boston area NOAA Weather Radio signal is on 162.475 MHz from Blue Hill, Massachusetts. NOAA Weather Radio uses seven VHF channels between 162.400 and 162.550 MHz.

    What is marine VHF channel 16?

    Channel 16, 156.800 MHz, is the international distress, safety and calling channel. The US Coast Guard announces safety broadcasts on 16 and then reads them on channel 22A.
